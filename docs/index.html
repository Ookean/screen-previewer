ScreenPreview for Windows

A lightweight live preview for any of your monitors, with window layouts and zoom for the screen you can't see.

What it does

ScreenPreview shows a live, resizable preview of any monitor connected to your PC in a single small window. It's built for setups where a monitor is set to "Extend" and isn't visible on your main screen, so you can see what's on it without physically looking at it or switching inputs.

Pick a monitor from the dropdown, pick any open window, and send it over. You can also control how it lands and zoom into it, all from the same small window.

Features

  • Live monitor preview — watch any connected display in a resizable window, and switch between monitors with a dropdown.
  • Move windows between monitors — send any open window to the selected monitor, centered, keeping its size.
  • Window layouts — choose Center, Maximize, or snap to the left, right, top or bottom half, then press Apply layout. You can re-arrange a window that is already on the other screen.
  • Zoom the shared window — zoom in, zoom out or reset to 100% on the window you sent to the other screen, without leaving the preview.

Zoom works with apps that use Ctrl + / Ctrl − to zoom, such as browsers, image viewers, PDF readers and most Office apps.

Built at the game table

ScreenPreview started while playing D&D. A Dungeon Master sharing a screen with the players, such as a map, a handout or a monster portrait on a second display facing the table, can't see that display without turning around. And once something is on it, you often need to make it fill the screen or zoom in on a detail.

So the DM keeps the preview open on their own screen. They see exactly what the players see, move the right window onto the player display, set it to full screen or half screen, and zoom in on part of a map, all without leaving their seat or interrupting the game.

It's just as useful outside the game table, and that's why it isn't built only for D&D.

Use cases

  • Tabletop / DM setups — run a secondary player-facing display, arrange and zoom what's on it, and check it without turning around.
  • Extra display you can't see — preview a monitor facing away from you, in another room, or otherwise out of view.
  • Presenting or streaming — check exactly what's on your presentation or capture display before switching to it.
  • Multi-monitor window wrangling — quickly move a stray window onto the right monitor and snap it into place without dragging it across the desktop.
